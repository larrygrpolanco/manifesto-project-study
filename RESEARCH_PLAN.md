First, the good news: the plan's numbers are faithful to MLB

Your plan's "17 GB / 12 NZ retained" is right, and your instinct to treat full-pool-vs-retained as a blocking decision is well-founded — the screen is arbitrary (their word) and reliability-based, so it directly shapes every entropy value.

So the bloat you're feeling is not the plan inflating its foundation. The foundation is solid. The bloat is something else, and I think it's diagnostic.
Second: the bloat is the plan compensating for unmeasured assumptions
Re-reading with the source in hand, I notice the plan's prose gets most elaborate exactly where its evidence is thinnest. The longest, most rhetorically loaded passages — "the hinge of the paper," "or Beat 3 dies," "the danger zone" — are all attached to quantities nobody has computed yet: how many sentences land in the false-consensus cell, whether the company×size grid actually separates, whether RILE drift exceeds the noise MLB already documented. The plan is using narrative confidence as a placeholder for empirical confidence. That's why it reads as bloated: it's arguing for results instead of reporting them, because it can't report them yet.
So yes — your read is exactly right, and sharper than "maybe I should pilot." The bloat is a symptom telling you which assumptions are load-bearing-but-unverified. Strip the rhetoric and the plan is making four empirical bets:

Enough sentences are human-ambiguous to populate the danger cell. (Checkable now, no API.)
Models cluster by company, not just size or noise. (Needs a small model pilot.)
Models agree where humans split — and do so directionally. (Needs both profiles.)
That directional lean survives aggregation into RILE beyond the centrist attenuation MLB already proved exists. (This is the one I'd worry about most — see below.)

Third: the source quietly raises the bar for your Beat 5
This is the most important thing I found, and it wasn't in the plan. MLB already demonstrated that misclassification distorts RILE — and they showed it's primarily a centrist attenuation that flattens extreme scores toward zero, present even when the misclassification matrix is strictly symmetric, and at reliability ~0.5 it cuts the estimate of a true rile value of −50 or 50 almost in half.
Your Beat 5 claims something different and stronger: not symmetric attenuation but directional capture — models leaning one way on a class of ambiguous boundaries, moving RILE in a signed direction that doesn't cancel. That's a real, novel claim. But it now has to clear a bar the plan doesn't acknowledge: you have to show the model-induced shift is directional and separable from the centrist attenuation MLB says is already there by default. With n=2 documents, you almost certainly can't establish that as prevalence — only as the GB-067-style existence proof. The plan half-knows this but its title ("silent capture") still promises the stronger thing.
So: what the pilot is actually for
Don't pilot to "see what happens." Pilot to resolve the four bets above in the cheapest possible order — and notice the first one needs no models at all:
Step 0 (tonight, no API): compute the human ambiguity profile and count the danger cell. You have the logs on disk. Before any model runs, you can answer: of 179 sentences, how many are genuinely human-split (entropy above some threshold)? If that's 60+, the design has room. If it's 15–20, Beat 4 is thin and you should know that before spending on 45k calls. This single computation also forces your two blocking decisions (000 handling, retained-vs-full-pool), because both change this count.
Step 1 (small, cheap): 3–4 models from 2 companies, temp 1, the ~15 highest-human-entropy sentences. This tells you whether the entropy-vs-entropy signal exists at all and whether anything clusters by company — the two bets that, if they fail, kill the paper regardless of scale. ~200 calls, not 45k.
